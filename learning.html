The most important part of your journey in learning guitar is practice. The absolute only way to improve is true, structured practice and correcting your mistakes. Playing for fun is good too, and it's why we get into this hobby to begin with, but structured practice will take you so much further with noticeable improvement. Here is a recommendation on a practice schedule for those of you who just got your guitar today and want to start messing around!

Exercise Notes Time
Tuning Take as long as you need, make sure each note is in tune with standard tuning. As long as it takes
Spider Exercise Play the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th frets starting at the top string with your pointer, middle, ring, and pinky fingers on each fret, moving onto the next string when you are finished with one. Take your time, play them cleanly. Once you play the last note, start going backwards from highest to lowest, 4th, 3rd, 2nd, 1st, change string, so on so forth. 7 mins 30 secs
Spider Exercise To Metronome Same process as Spider Exercise, but turn on a metronome. Play along to each click, letting the note ring then letting the next play on time. Getting a good sense of timing makes all the difference. Set it to a tempo you can do, but also challenges you. Don't try to go too fast right off the rip. The key, just like with working out at a gym, is to challenge yourself but don't overdo it. 7 mins 30 secs

This of course is to just get you started. Remember, it's ok to fail! It's ok to not be amazing right off the rip! Just like when you work out and get sore the next day, failure is just preparation for tomorrow's success. Even 15 minutes is enough time to get daily improvement!

You have reached the end of this website, and thank you so much for sticking it out to here, I hope you continue to chase higher heights, because music is amazing, and being able to make it is even better. Before I leave off, there are a couple of sources I can recommend!

Justin Sandercoe, i.e. JustinGuitar on YouTube has an amazing beginners guide that is far more comprehensive than my own and provides more of a structured method to learn, I recommend looking into his beginner playlist on YouTube. It's free, and nothing's better than free!

If you want something even more structured, a website I have been using is pickupmusic.com which is a subscription based learning website with good instructors, good community, and you can even get feedback!

However, if you have the money and live close enough to such resources, finding an in-person instructor is the absolute best way to learn. An in-person teacher can see your mistakes in real time, cater to your skill level, and work alongside you to give you the best value for your money.

No matter the route you take, stick to it. You got this, rockstar.
